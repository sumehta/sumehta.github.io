Sneha Mehta

I train and evaluate language and multimodal models that run in products used by hundreds of millions of people, most recently Meta AI on Ray-Ban Smart Glasses and Quest and VR devices, and before that TikTok Search and Twitter.

I started on feature-film pipelines at DreamWorks Animation, earned a Ph.D. in machine learning at Virginia Tech, and now work on post-training, RL, and agentic systems.

I also write about AI research on my blog, tech_muse on Substack.

Research interests

My work has spanned three ways machines make sense of the world: language, from LLM post-training and agentic tool use to information extraction and translation; vision, from multimodal assistants and video understanding to face recognition; and graphics, rendering and simulating worlds for feature animation.

Next, I want to bring these together in world models: systems that understand the world the way people do, by combining sight, sound, language, and other senses into a single picture they can reason about, predict from, and act in.

Experience

  1. 2025

    Meta

    Language & LLMsVision & Multimodal

    I worked on post-training the multimodal models behind Meta AI, the assistant on Ray-Ban Smart Glasses. My focus was teaching the assistant to understand what you see and turn it into the right action. On VR, I worked on agentic modeling: curating training data, designing evaluations, and running online reinforcement learning. The goal was an assistant that can reliably use tools, control the device, and adapt to the person it's talking with.

  2. 2023

    TikTok

    Vision & MultimodalSearch & Ranking

    On TikTok Search, I brought vision-language models into video understanding. I fine-tuned VLMs to summarize what happens in a video so search could use that signal directly, and the summaries became a content signal used across retrieval and ranking.

  3. 2021

    Twitter

    Language & LLMsSearch & Ranking

    At Twitter I worked at the intersection of NLP and recommendation. I built the named-entity recognition models behind an end-to-end entity linking platform that served Trends and the Public API, and explored knowledge distillation to make those models efficient. I also reworked how conversations were ranked, bringing in new architectures and personalization across several production models.

  4. Summers 2018 & 2019

    Netflix

    Language & LLMs

    Over two summers I worked on machine translation for subtitles. Off-the-shelf translation struggles with complex sentences in low-resource languages, so I developed a Transformer-based approach that simplifies the source text before translation. That work became an AAAI paper [1] and was featured on NVIDIA's technical blog [2].

  5. 2015

    Virginia Tech

    Language & LLMsVision & Multimodal

    I graduated with a Ph.D. from the Data Mining Group at Virginia Tech, where I had the privilege to be advised by Dr. Naren Ramakrishnan. I was also fortunate enough to have had my Ph.D. studies funded by both an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship and a DARPA Research Grant. My thesis explored an attention-based multi-instance learning approach for interpretable event detection and key-phrase identification, along with reading-comprehension-based event extraction [1, 2, 3, 4]. I also worked on generating natural-language narratives from large causal graphs for DARPA's World Modelers program. Before that, I spent time developing crowd+AI systems with Dr. Kurt Luther, including Civil War Photo Sleuth, which combines face recognition with crowdsourced expertise to identify people in historical photographs.

  6. 2014

    DreamWorks Animation

    Computer Graphics

    I started my career in feature animation. I built pieces of a distributed compute framework that orchestrates the large rendering and simulation workflows behind each film, and worked as a Layout TD on Penguins of Madagascar.
